# Story discipline

## Never narrate what the recording does not show

If the video does not show it, cut the sentence. A narration that describes a
capability while the screen shows something else is the fastest way to lose a
judge's trust, and it is unrecoverable once lost.

## The IBM Bob section is mandatory

The rules require the video to demonstrate the solution **and** explain how
IBM Bob was used. Name the modes, the skills, the personas, the MCP server.
Show `.bob/` on screen. A brilliant demo missing this section is
non-compliant.

## Do not fabricate a Bob interaction

Not for timing, not for a cleaner take, not because the real one was slow.
Record the real thing. If it is too slow, cut to it, do not stage it.

## Claim only what is implemented

No enterprise SSO, no multi-cluster, no production RBAC, no Prometheus, no
autonomous healing, unless the code does it. Positioning:

> A human-controlled Kubernetes incident response agent that turns fragmented
> operational evidence and tickets into an explainable remediation workflow
> using IBM Bob.

Not a platform. Not a chatbot. Not another kubectl wrapper.

## Structure

Open with the app working, then break it. Never open with an architecture
diagram — that is where most teams lose the room, in the first fifteen
seconds.

## Let the moments land

Two seconds of silence after the successful booking. Do not talk over
recovery, and do not talk over the recorded rejection. Those are the two shots
the whole video exists to deliver.

## Write for the eleventh submission of the day

Short sentences. One idea each. No throat-clearing. The judge is tired and the
first ten seconds decide whether they watch properly or skim.
